The AES Standard

The Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) is the global gold standard for symmetric encryption. Established in 2001, it is used by the U.S. government, banks, and secure applications worldwide to protect top-secret data.

How AES Works

1. The Block Cipher

Unlike the Caesar cipher which scrambles one letter at a time, AES is a Block Cipher. It chops your plaintext message up into blocks of exactly 128 bits. It then mathematically scrambles each block as a single unit.

2. The "Rounds"

AES doesn't just scramble the data once. It runs the block through a washing machine of mathematical operations (Substitute, Shift, Mix, Add Key) either 10, 12, or 14 times in a row, depending on how large the key is.

The Key Distribution Problem

Symmetric algorithms like AES are mathematically unbreakable. But they all share one fatal flaw: how do you safely give the secret key to the receiver without a hacker stealing it first?

Enter Diffie-Hellman

In 1976, Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman solved this problem with pure mathematics. They invented a protocol that allows two people who have never met to generate a shared secret password in broad daylight, even if a hacker is listening to every single word they say.

Important: Diffie-Hellman is purely a Key Exchange Protocol used to safely establish the password that AES will use later!

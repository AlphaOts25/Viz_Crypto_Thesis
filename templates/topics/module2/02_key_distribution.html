Topic 3

The Key Distribution Problem

Symmetric algorithms like AES are mathematically unbreakable. But they all share one fatal flaw: how do you safely give the secret key to the receiver without a hacker stealing it first?

The Catch-22 of Cryptography

Imagine Alice wants to send her credit card number to Bob (a server like Amazon). She encrypts it with AES. The ciphertext is secure. But for Bob to read it, Alice must also send him the key.

Meet Eve (The Eavesdropper)

Eve is a hacker secretly listening to all traffic on the network. If Alice sends the secret key to Bob over the internet, Eve will copy it. Once Eve has the key, the unbreakable AES algorithm becomes useless.

You can't encrypt the key before sending it, because then you'd need a second key to unlock the first key, creating an endless loop. This problem stumped scientists for thousands of years.

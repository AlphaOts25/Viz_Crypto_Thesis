Plaintext vs. Ciphertext

Before we can understand complex cryptographic algorithms, we must understand the core goal of cryptography: protecting information by changing its state.

The Fundamentals

Plaintext

Plaintext is any unencrypted data. It is the original, readable message. This doesn't just mean text files; an unencrypted image, a database of passwords, or a network request are all considered "plaintext" if anyone intercepting them can read them perfectly.

"Hello, meet me at 9 PM."

Ciphertext

Ciphertext is the scrambled, unreadable output of an encryption algorithm. To a computer or an attacker intercepting the data, ciphertext looks completely random. It contains the original data, but it is locked away.

U2FsdGVkX19z... (Random Data)

The process of turning plaintext into ciphertext is called Encryption. The process of turning ciphertext back into plaintext is called Decryption.

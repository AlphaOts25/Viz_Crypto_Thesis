Topic 1

Encryption and Decryption

Before learning how encryption works, it is important to understand the difference between plaintext and ciphertext. These two terms describe the state of data before and after it has been protected. Plaintext is the original message or information that can be easily read and understood. Ciphertext is the protected version of that message after it has been changed by an encryption process.

The Fundamentals

The main purpose of encryption is to turn plaintext into ciphertext so that sensitive information remains protected. The reverse process is called decryption, where ciphertext is changed back into readable plaintext. This relationship is the foundation of cryptography: plaintext is the original data, encryption protects it, ciphertext hides it, and decryption restores it.

Plaintext

Plaintext can be anything that contains readable information. It may be a text message, password, email, image, file, database record, or network request. If the data can be understood without any special process, it is considered plaintext. For example, the message “Hello, meet me at 9 PM” is plaintext because anyone who sees it can immediately understand its meaning.

"Hello, meet me at 9 PM."

Ciphertext

Ciphertext is the result of applying encryption to plaintext. It appears scrambled, random, or meaningless to anyone who does not have the correct key or method to unlock it. Even if an attacker intercepts the ciphertext, they should not be able to understand the original message. For example, the same message may become something like “U2FsdGVkX19z...” after encryption.

U2FsdGVkX19z... (Random Data)

The process of turning plaintext into ciphertext is called Encryption. The process of turning ciphertext back into plaintext is called Decryption.

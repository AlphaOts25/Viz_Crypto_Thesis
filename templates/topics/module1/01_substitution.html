The Caesar Cipher

Named after Julius Caesar, who used it to communicate with his generals, the Caesar Cipher is one of the simplest and most widely known encryption techniques. It is a type of substitution cipher where each letter in the plaintext is replaced by a letter some fixed number of positions down the alphabet.

For example, with a shift of 3, A would be replaced by D, B would become E, and so on. The method wraps around the alphabet, meaning Z becomes C. While easy to crack today, it formed the foundation for centuries of cryptographic advancements!

The Caesar Cipher

The Caesar Cipher is one of the earliest and simplest methods used to protect written messages. It works by replacing each letter in the original message with another letter that is a fixed number of positions away in the alphabet. For example, if the shift value is 3, the letter A becomes D, B becomes E, and C becomes F.

This method shows the basic idea of encryption: changing readable information into a form that is harder to understand. Although the Caesar Cipher is no longer secure for modern use, it is still important because it helps explain how substitution, shifting, keys, encryption, and decryption work in a simple and visual way. the earliest and simplest methods used to protect written messages.

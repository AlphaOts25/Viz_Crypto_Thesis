Module 3 - Topic 2

The Man-in-the-Middle Attack

Public Key Cryptography defeats passive listeners. But what if Eve isn't just listening? What if she has the power to actively intercept, block, and replace the messages traveling across the network?

The Fake Padlock

A Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) attack happens during the very first step of communication: the key exchange. Because the internet is fundamentally anonymous, Alice has no mathematical way to prove the public key she received actually belongs to Bob.

  • Step 1: Alice asks for Bob's public key.
  • Step 2: Eve intercepts the request. Eve generates her own keypair.
  • Step 3: Eve gives her public key to Alice, pretending to be Bob. (Alice is tricked!)
  • Step 4: Alice locks a message with Eve's key. Eve intercepts it, unlocks it, changes the message, locks it with Bob's real public key, and forwards it to Bob.

The Result: Both Alice and Bob think they are having a secure, encrypted conversation with each other. In reality, Eve is secretly reading and altering every single word.

The Diffie-Hellman Exchange

How can Alice and Bob agree on a secret password while shouting to each other across a crowded room where everyone (including Eve) can hear them? The answer is a one-way mathematical function.

The Paint Mixing Metaphor

In cryptography, we use complex math (like prime numbers and modulos) to act as a one-way function. A one-way function is something that is easy to do, but almost impossible to undo.

The best way to understand this is by thinking about mixing paint:

  • Easy to mix: If you mix Yellow and Blue, you easily get Green.
  • Hard to un-mix: If someone hands you a bucket of Green paint, it is physically impossible to separate it back out into the exact original Yellow and Blue drops.

Diffie-Hellman uses this "un-mixable" math to let Alice and Bob generate a shared secret key over a public internet connection, completely locking out Eve!
